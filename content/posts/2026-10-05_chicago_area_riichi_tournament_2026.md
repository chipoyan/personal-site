## miscellaneous thoughts

saikouisen sho-chombo rules are pretty harsh.

shown-tile "misehai" was 50-50 on whether my tables reported it or not. each such event is supposed to be a warning, and 3 warnings is a sho-chombo. I don't think any of my tables got up to an entire 3, fortunately.

I'm personally not very careful when hand-shuffling, but it seems like there is a proper technique to it. I feel like there was a decent amount of variance in the shuffling techniques that people used, so I wonder if there's a way to get it more standardized.

I thought it was amusing that one of the tournament staff guys was dressed in a formal suit and there was the other guy in the vtuber haori.

staying in the hotel and being able to just walk down to the tournament room was nice, but was it worth the ~$300, when I am pretty sure my mom's friend over in palatine would have been down to let me stay in their guest room for free, probably not.

we fell about a half hour behind schedule day 1, but after the staff learned the spreadsheet they were using or whatever it was got resolved, day 2 went much better. pretty well organized overall otherwise, I feel like people were on top of things.

### day 1

I couldn't sleep until around 2 am, so that was unfortunate, but I didn't feel it in the morning at all.

#### game 1

after winning a cheap hand to repeat as dealer, I got chiitoi tenpai with a hell wait on the south wind dora, which I didn't riichi. after a couple of turns, my toimen called kan on the 4m, and then declared riichi a few turns after that. I drew the double musuji 4p on the ippatsu turn and folded, since I thought there were too many dead suji, and I had a bit of a lead, but then the very next turn, I drew the south! and then the game went to ryuukyoku and it turned out his wait was 258p. I probably should have just declared riichi when I got into tenpai, gambling on the chance at an 18k hand. in a tournament, where there's not much difference between scoring slightly above average and striking out, I think it would have been better to take a big swing there. unless maybe I had a bigger lead than I did.

I later had another good chance to win the game, with another chiitoi hand, but I fucked up my tanki selection.
toimen had already declared riichi, and I got into tenpai with a choice between 6s tanki or 8m tanki. since my shimocha was cutting a lot of middle souzu tiles early, I judged that 6s and 8m were probably about equal in terms of wait quality. 8m is further out to the edge, so it's harder to use, but there was evidence that 6s wasn't in shimocha's hand. then, if the wait quality is about the same, it's better to declare riichi by discarding the 8m, keeping the 6s, since 6s is more likely to deal in than 8m. but in the end, toimen got a direct hit off me for a mangan after discarding the 8m, off a tile that I don't remember but it definitely wasn't the 6s.

in the end, my toimen got first with about 50k points, and I got second with maybe around 30k points. I should clarify here that 30k points is the starting score, unlike in mahjong soul.

#### game 5

I had to pay out for mangan tsumo as dealer twice.

other than that, I had a dama mangan waiting on genbutsu to the riichi, but dealt into that riichi with the 9m.

I actually had a choice there between cutting 9m and cutting 6m to take that tenpai. it is well known that if the riichi player has cut 3m before the riichi, then 6m is safer to discard than 9m. we can see this by listing out the waits that 6m can deal into vs the waits that 9m can deal into. for 6m: 78m, 57m, 45m, 66mXX shanpon, 6m tanki. but it can't be 45m, since it's furiten, and 57m is unlikely, since you normally wouldn't cut 3m from 357m so early. so the revised list is: 78m, 66mXX shanpon, 6m tanki. then, for 9m: 78m, 99mXX shanpon, 9m tanki. so this is the same number of possibilities, but then the 9m shanpon and tanki are more dangerous than the 6m shanpon and tanki, since, uh, I'm not actually 100% sure on this part.
some combination of the tiles near the edge being better waits, so people are more likely to try to wait on them, and also the middle tiles have a slightly higher likelihood of being inside of the other players' hands if you can't see them, but I'm not 100% sure about that second part.

so I could have cut 6m there, but since 9m was discarded once while the 6m was live, I thought it would be better to cut 9m. this also lets you cut 8m in the future if you draw 5m or 0m. one final factor, if I did cut 6m, even though it's the "standard" play when 3m is cut, I thought it might still look like more of a push to the other players compared to if I cut 9m, and clue them into my dama hand. so I cut 9m and dealt into the riichi player's 9m tanki. this was actually a rare 1 han 70 fu hand, so we got to spend a minute or so discussing how to score it.

## concluding thoughts

I am pretty happy that I didn't have any chombos.

I went 221134241 for a 2.22 average, which is about the same as my alt account's joukyuu fast tonpuu stats. it's significantly better than my jade room average. I imagine that with the swiss system that matches you against players with a similar score to you that the overall skill level you can expect to go up against if you're winning games is closer to jade than joukyuu, so I think I probably got lucky overall this tournament, just not quite lucky enough.

honestly I can't say it was all that great of a time, for the amount that you're paying for it and the hotel and travel and everything. I'll probably still go again next year.
if I had just gotten one sick discard read off or similar, I think I'd have felt pretty satisfied. sadly, I did not. actually, the whole tournament, I felt like I wasn't really able to track tedashi and tsumogiri well. probably since I haven't been playing enough. I've been doing all sorts of stuff other than grinding ranked mahjong soul games as of late, setting up this blog and writing this post included, but also just wasting a lot of time. I personally kind of doubt that grinding ladder games is all that important in becoming a better player, but at least in that facet of the game, I was definitely feeling the rust.
this rust is a pretty big problem, since if I can't track tedashi and tsumogiri, then doing reads is impossible, and if I can't hit discard reads, the only way that mahjong is making me happy is pretty much if I win, by which I mean at least making the semifinal tables. or else if I dealt into a yakuman or something, that could have been a better story. shout outs to shiratsuki shino from shinohayu, my favorite saki character. she likes playing mahjong and finds it fun even if she's losing. I should reread shinohayu.

I definitely felt pretty down for a couple of days after the tournament, since I felt going into it like I was pretty hot shit and it turns out I'm not. well, it's been a couple of weeks since then, and I have gotten over it and accepted it. I mean, come on, I barely played any games this whole year. also, one of the things that happened was that I played against a tenhou 9d guy, so that had me shook since I figured that at least in terms of online mahjong there still aren't that many people better than me, but now there's this guy and navitas at 9d? it's over, I lost - was my initial reaction, but reflecting deeper on it, wasn't that probably just navitas? that was probably just navitas, wasn't it. like, there's not that many 9ds out there. what the hell, I should have shaken his hand and gotten his autograph and everything. and also, what do I mean "I lost", it's not some competition! lol.

having actually gone out to this event and played these hands and made these mistakes, I do feel like I have learned some lessons. collecting everything together:

- riichi with chiitoi hell wait honor tile dora tanki.
- don't call with slow hands if other players are already dropping akadora.
- have a plan for what happens if you draw the non-winning tile of your atozuke.
- do the right thing in each of those other relatively rare tile efficiency scenarios above.
- bring your own food and caffeine, and probably alcohol too. one of the UW club guys brought a lunchbox, which I think is just obviously a good idea. as for caffeine, I think I actually paid 9 bucks for a celsius can on day 2. total madness.
- get I-pass, it's apparently free but you have to deposit some toll money up front. but that's just money that I'd be spending anyway.
- play way more leading up to the tournament.
