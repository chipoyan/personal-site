every once in a while, I see people say that pinfu is a reward for tile efficiency, or that it’s somehow fundamental, like if you understand pinfu then you understand mahjong. but it’s really easy to think of examples where the most efficient tile to cut is different from the tile you’d cut to go for pinfu.

I don’t actually know if the remarks I see represent some sort of real faction of pinfu theorists or some sort of real tendency in the populace to perceive pinfu as having more significance than it really does, or if it’s just some random people being wrong on the internet. so, I might be arguing against a position that nobody actually holds, but I still think that it will be educational to go through every reason it’s wrong to say that pinfu is a reward for tile efficiency, even if you agree completely with my thesis.

## pinfu is not a reward for tile efficiency

in the following hands, what discard is most efficient, and what discard is best to maximize pinfu?

![111345m23478p246s](../../img/pinfu/1.png)

efficiency: 26s, pinfu: 1m

but you would never cut 1m from this hand.

![345m2378p234s3355z](../../img/pinfu/2.png)

supposing that the west is not yakuhai, there's no doubt that discarding west increases your chances of winning your hand compared to cutting the haku. but the haku pair is the tile you have to get rid of for pinfu.

you could cut either the west or the haku or even a ryanmen here, depending on how many dora you have in hand and how the round seems to be proceeding.

![11345m23378p34s78s](../../img/pinfu/3.png)

efficiency: cut any of the incomplete blocks here other than 233p, pinfu: cut 3p

cutting 3p here actually maximizes ukeire. but it's better to cut a ryanmen so that your 1-shanten ukeire is maximized, since going from 1-shanten to tenpai is more of a bottleneck. this is the "1-shanten peak theory". you might still cut 3p in a south 4 situation where you want to avoid riichi.

![11345m23378p34s68s](../../img/pinfu/4.png)

efficiency: cut 68s, pinfu: cut 3p

replace one of the ryanmens from last hand with a kanchan just to hammer the point in.

![11345m234678p4s79s](../../img/pinfu/5.png)

efficiency: riichi discarding 4s, pinfu: cut 9s

with any dora, riichi is much better than returning to iishanten. without dora, riichi is still better, but it's marginal.

from [uchikawa’s book](https://www.amazon.com/Riichi-Mahjong-Essential-Playbook-Professional-ebook/dp/B0H98QW634), in chapter 2, "plays for pinfu",

with 9p as the dora:
![340m233p799p4556s tsumo 6m](../../img/pinfu/6.png)

efficiency: 7p, pinfu: 3p

uchikawa recommends cutting 3p here, since the ukeire is higher by cutting 3p. I will add in that cutting 3p increases your chances of getting pinfu, and then if you can riichi and tsumo, then adding pinfu and 3 dora to that gets you a haneman.

but cutting 7p maximizes your ukeire at iishanten, so I still think it's better to cut 7p.

anyway, I think it is clear from looking at these examples that there are times when one discard is better for pinfu, and one is better from a tile efficiency perspective.

## riichi is a reward for tile efficiency

we can argue that riichi is a reward for tile efficiency because if you play with good efficiency, you can get closed hand tenpai more often, which lets you riichi more often.

it is more difficult to argue that pinfu is a reward for good tile efficiency. obviously, there’s all those counterexamples above, but also, just try and construct a positive argument for why pinfu is a reward for tile efficiency. I can’t come up with much here other than that it encourages you to wait on ryanmen.

## argument from authority

look at matsumoto’s youtube series with haneru
look at the disdain he has when the guests say that pinfu is their favorite yaku

on the other hand, look at who we have on the “pinfu is the reward for tile efficiency” side - absolutely zero mahjongers of note.

sakurai and tsuchida don’t favor pinfu as a yaku either.

## true nature of pinfu

pinfu is the yaku you get when you don’t score any fu past the base 20 you start with.

well, except for the fu you get from calling ron with a closed hand. and also if you win by tsumo, that’s 2 fu if you don’t have a pinfu hand, but if you do have pinfu, then you score pinfu tsumo and remain at 20 fu.

why does an ankou or yakuhai pair in your hand turn pinfu off? since it gives you fu, there you go.
now try and explain why a yakuhai pair is incompatible with pinfu in terms of tile efficiency.
this is difficult to do. I take this to mean that pinfu is more "about" fu than it is "about" tile efficiency.

## what is to be done

stop teaching pinfu to beginners. especially with explanations that don’t mention the concept of fu. I imagine that the creators of such explanations want to try to keep things simpler, but they are really putting the cart before the horse.

instead, mahjong players can learn about pinfu when they learn how to count fu and score hands, and then an understanding of pinfu can come for free.
